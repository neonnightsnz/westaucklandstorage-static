# West Auckland Storage — Brand Voice & Visual Identity Guide

## 1. Positioning

West Auckland Storage is not trying to be the biggest storage brand in Auckland. It is trying to be the most trusted local one. Every decision — language, colour, layout, imagery — should make a West Auckland boat owner feel like they have found the practical, honest, local option: the yard that knows what it is storing and does not pretend to be a marina.

**Honest operating truths**

- **Location:** 20 Akatea Rd, Glendene, Auckland 0602
- **Phone:** 09 818 4586
- **Description:** Reliable and affordable boat, caravan and vehicle storage.
- **The yard:** Span Farm Boat Yard Ltd — a real working boat yard in Glendene.
- **Key differentiators:** location, price, and slipway access.

## 2. Target Audience

Primary groups:

1. **Boat owners in West Auckland** — Launching regularly, need storage close to home, often frustrated by marina costs and marina waitlists.
2. **Caravan and trailer owners** — Seasonal users who want a secure home for the caravan or trailer between trips.
3. **General vehicle storage** — Cars, 4WDs, utes, trucks, motorbikes, jet skis, kayaks and equipment.
4. **Long-term and seasonal storage** — Owners who need somewhere reliable for months at a time.

All groups share one thing: **they value proximity, price, and security.** All share one fear: **paying too much to store something they are not using.**

## 3. Brand Personality

- **Practical.** No fluff. The yard speaks in plain terms about what it stores and what it costs.
- **Local.** A West Auckland business for West Auckland people. Neighbourly, not corporate.
- **Honest.** Does not overpromise. Does not exaggerate security or facilities.
- **Reliable.** It turns up, it does the job, it does not let people down.
- **Unpretentious.** No marble floors. No champagne. Just a good yard with fair prices.

## 4. Voice and Tone

**Voice characteristics:**

- Direct and conversational — write like a person, not a brochure.
- Confident but not boastful.
- Warm but not overly familiar.
- Encouraging but never pushy.

**What to avoid:**

- Corporate language (e.g. "solutions provider", "value-add", "tailored outcomes").
- Over-selling or inflated claims.
- Jargon and storage-industry buzzwords.
- Overly formal constructions.

## 5. Messaging Hierarchy

1. **Lead with the guest need** — Where will I keep this thing I am not using?
2. **Follow with the real difference** — Location. Price. Slipway.
3. **Close with the easy next step** — Get a quote, call the yard, ask about space.

## 6. Core Messages

**Primary tagline options:**

- "Keep it close. Keep it sorted."
- "Store it local. Store it sorted."
- "Your West Auckland storage."

**Supporting messages:**

- "No marina prices. No marina waitlist."
- "Close to home. Flexible terms."
- "Boat, caravan and vehicle storage in Glendene."

**Audience-specific:**

- **Boat owners:** "Why pay marina prices to store your boat?" / "Your boat, your spot, close to home."
- **Caravan and trailer:** "Give the caravan a home between trips." / "Room to store when you are not on the road."
- **General vehicle storage:** "Somewhere safe for the things you do not use every day."
- **Long-term and seasonal:** "Store smarter, not somewhere further away."

Only use figures where they remain accurate — check before publishing:

- Company incorporated on 10 January 2008, confirmed by the owner. Use "Company established in 2008" in short copy. Incorporation does not establish continuous storage operations.
- 100+ boats currently in storage

## 7. Content Principles

- Write at a reading level that a West Auckland boat owner would find natural.
- Use active voice. ("We store your boat", not "Boats can be stored.")
- Keep sentences short and paragraphs to 2–4 sentences.
- Use the second person. ("Your boat", "your space", "your spot.")
- Use real numbers where possible, and never fake ones.
- Include a clear call to action on every page.
- Describe slipway access conditionally when the current operational status is not confirmed.

## 8. Words and Phrases

**Always use:** genuine, secure, no fuss, our yard, close to home, straightforward, your spot, sorted, reliable, affordable

**Never use:** premier, state-of-the-art, world-class, luxury, seamless, cutting-edge, unrivalled, industry-leading

Treat these with caution, and only say them when verified: "secure", "24/7", "undercover", "alarmed", "brand new"

## 9. Visual Identity

**The industrial wordmark** — A custom, uppercase, condensed sans-serif display face. Tall, narrow, bold, in the spirit of Impact or Bebas Neue. The logotype is set tight and does not use italics or script.

**Typography pairing:**

- **Display:** condensed industrial sans for the wordmark and headings.
- **Body:** a clean workhorse grotesk for body copy. Avoid decorative or novelty faces.

**Colour palette:**

- Primary: `#145693` (deep marine blue)
- Bright accent: `#128BFB` (working blue — CTAs, links, markings)
- Light accent: `#A5D2FD` (sky blue)
- Base tints: white, light grey, near-black
- Keep the palette restricted to marine blues and quiet neutrals.
- Do not introduce decorative golds, greens, reds, gradients, or luxury-marina colours.

**Imagery direction:**

- Use real photography of the yard, stored boats, caravans, trailers and vehicles.
- Wide, practical, documentary framing. Overcast light is fine; the yard is outdoors.
- Avoid generic stock imagery and padlock/key iconography.
- Do not photograph anything that overstates the site's security or facilities.

## 10. Design Principles

- Practical and purposeful — every element should be easy to read and easy to use.
- Sturdy and industrial — the branding should feel like a working yard, not a spa.
- Maritime but grounded — hint at the water without going nautical kitsch.
- Local and unpretentious — it should feel like a business in Glendene, not a global brand.

## 11. Applications

- **Website header** should carry the wordmark clearly, with phone number and quote CTA always reachable.
- **Signage** should use the wordmark plus phone number; keep it legible from the road.
- **Google Business Profile** should carry the same images and the same tone as the site.
- **Quotes and invoices** should be plain, clear and consistent with the brand colours.

## 12. Final Checklist

Before publishing anything, ask:

- Does it sound like a real West Auckland boat yard?
- Would a boat owner trust it enough to call?
- Is the message about them, not us?
- Have we avoided promising security, facilities, or services that are not currently in place?
- Is the next step obvious?
- Would the owner look at it and say "yes, that is us"?
