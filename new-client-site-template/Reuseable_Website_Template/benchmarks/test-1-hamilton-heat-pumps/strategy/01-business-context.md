## Observations from the existing site

1. **Templated copy.** The homepage contains "customer focused" three times in
   different constructions, including the ungrammatical "an customer focused
   solution". This reads as generated filler, not a local business writing
   about its own work.
2. **No local specificity.** Nothing about Hamilton's climate (frost, winter
   humidity), local housing stock, or the Waikato. Competitor Aeon Energy does
   this well; it is a demonstrated differentiator.
3. **Brands not claimed.** The brief names Mitsubishi Electric and Daikin, but
   the homepage does not present dealer or certified-installer status. If that
   status exists, the site is hiding its strongest credential.
4. **Thin Location pages.** `/hamilton/fairfield` presents a service list
   under the heading "Find Fairfield Heat Pumps By Service" with no
   Fairfield-specific content.
5. **No visible pricing.** Competitors publish price bands ($2,500–$4,500 for a
   split system). This is a demonstrable trust and conversion advantage.

## Market context (verified)

- **Pricing:** single split system $2,000–$4,500 installed; multi-head
  $5,000–$12,000+; ducted up to $20,000.
- **Warmer Kiwi Homes (EECA):** grants covering up to 90% of an approved heat
  pump, capped at **$3,450** incl. GST, for eligible homeowners (Community
  Services Card, SuperGold Combo, or low-income area). Only **high wall** heat
  pumps are funded; ducted and floor-mount are not.
- **Wood/pellet burner grants closed** to new applications on **9 January 2026**,
  which pushes remaining subsidy demand toward heat pumps.
