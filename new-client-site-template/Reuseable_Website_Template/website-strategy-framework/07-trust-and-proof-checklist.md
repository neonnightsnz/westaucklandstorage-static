# Stage 7: Trust and Proof Checklist

**Goal:** give local customers enough reassurance to enquire — using only
evidence that is real and verified.

## Why this stage exists

Local customers usually decide on trust before they decide on price. But
unverified claims are worse than no claims: they expose the business to
complaints, and Google's spam and content-quality systems actively discount
unsupported credibility signals.

> **Rule:** every claim must be verified by the business owner. Never publish
> unsupported ratings, response times, certifications, dealer status or warranty
> promises.

## E-E-A-T signals

Trust is one of four dimensions Google assesses. Cover all four:

- **Experience** — evidence the business has actually done this work: project
  photos, job specifics, "we have installed X of these" claims that are true.
- **Expertise** — qualifications, technical knowledge, correct terminology.
- **Authoritativeness** — reviews, citations, industry memberships, mentions
  elsewhere.
- **Trustworthiness** — accurate NAP, real contact details, transparent
  pricing or process, no exaggerated claims.

For team or author pages, add `ProfilePage` plus `Person` schema
(`name`, `jobTitle`, `worksFor`, `hasCredential`, `sameAs` to a
LinkedIn or professional profile).

## Review compliance

Reviews are the strongest local trust signal, but the rules are strict:

- **Review gating is prohibited.** You may not filter customers by sentiment
  before asking, or only ask happy customers.
- **Google's fake-engagement policy** prohibits incentivised, selective or
  fake reviews.
- **FTC Consumer Review Rule** (effective 21 October 2024) carries penalties up
  to **$53,088 per violation** in the US.
- Ask every customer, at the point of completion, the same way.
- Never write reviews on a customer's behalf.

## Substitution rules

If proof is missing, do not invent it. Choose an honest alternative:

| Missing | Instead of | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| No reviews yet | "5-star rated" | Show the process and real photos; ask recent customers |
| No certifications | Implied credentials | Describe the actual experience and training |
| No testimonials | Generic praise | Use project detail — what the job involved |
| No awards | "Award-winning" | State the years trading and specialism |

Flag every gap in the brief so it can be filled before launch.
