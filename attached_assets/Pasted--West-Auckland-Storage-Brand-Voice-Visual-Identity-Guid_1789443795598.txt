# West Auckland Storage

## Brand Voice & Visual Identity Guidelines

---

## Why This Document Exists

This document explains **how West Auckland Storage sounds, looks, and what makes it worth choosing.**

Use it when writing or designing:

* Website pages
* Google Business Profile content
* Quote forms and follow-up emails
* Signage
* Social content
* SEO and AI-search content
* Invoices and customer communications

The goal is simple: sound like Jeff and Andrew, run a straight operation, and never dress up a boat yard as something it isn't.

---

# 1. Core Positioning

West Auckland Storage (operating from Span Farm Boat Yard Ltd, 20 Akatea Road, Glendene, Waitakere) is secure, affordable outdoor storage for boats, caravans, trailers, vehicles and general recreational gear — close to home, with slipway access for smaller vessels.

We're not a marina. We're not a corporate self-storage chain. We're a working West Auckland boat yard that's been looking after people's boats and caravans for over 15 years, run by people who know what they're storing and why it matters to you.

---

# 2. Our Strongest Difference

## Location. Price. Slipway.

Not "premium waterfront facility" — the real guest benefit is:

**You can store your boat close to home, launch it easily, and not pay marina prices to do it.**

That matters particularly to:

* Boat owners without space at home
* Caravan and trailer owners between trips
* Trailer-boat owners under 8m who want to launch without the hassle
* People sick of marina waitlists and marina prices

Lead with the practical reason first, not the feature list.

### Good

"Marina too expensive, or the waitlist too long? Keep your boat close to home at Span Farm, and launch it straight off our slipway."

### Less useful

"Premium boat storage facility with slipway access."

The second sounds like a chain. The first sounds like West Auckland Storage.

# 3. Primary Guest Groups

## A. Boat Owners (Core Audience)

Trailer boats, launches, yachts, tinnies, jet skis — up to 24 tonne.

### What they want

* Somewhere secure and close to home
* To keep the boat out of the weather and off the driveway
* Easy in-and-out access when they want to use it
* A slipway that doesn't cost marina money
* Peace of mind over winter, or year-round

### Message to lead with

**Keep your boat close, safe, and easy to launch.**

---

## B. Caravan & Trailer Owners

Storing between trips, often longer-term.

### What they want

* Secure hardstand space
* Somewhere that frees up the driveway or garage at home
* Simple month-to-month arrangements
* Flexibility for short or long stays

### Message to lead with

**More space at home. Your caravan sorted here.**

---

## C. General Vehicle & Equipment Storage

Cars, 4WDs, utes, trucks, trailers, kayaks, surf gear, shipping containers.

### What they want

* An affordable, flexible outdoor space
* Straightforward monthly invoicing
* No fuss getting set up

### Message to lead with

**Flexible outdoor storage, sized to what you've actually got.**

---

## D. Long-Term & Seasonal Storers

Owners storing over winter, or for extended periods while a boat or caravan isn't in use.

### What they value

* Consistent security
* Protection from sun, weather, and other vehicles
* Lower risk of theft or vandalism
* Not having to think about it until they need it

### Message to lead with

**Store it right, forget about it, and it'll be ready when you are.**

# 4. The Experience We Are Selling

West Auckland Storage isn't selling square metres of hardstand.

The experience is:

**Straightforward. Secure. Close to home. No-nonsense.**

Guests should feel like they're dealing with people who actually run a boat yard — not a call centre.

That shows up in things like:

* A quick form or phone call to get a quote
* Finding your allocated spot with the team, not guessing
* Monthly invoicing that just works
* A team that knows boats and treats yours properly
* Slipway access that does the job without ceremony
* Flexible arrangements that adjust to what you actually need

The details may change over time (slipway repairs, new security systems, new facilities).

The underlying promise does not:

> **Your boat, caravan or gear is in safe hands, close to home, without the marina price tag.**

---

# 5. What Reviews Are Really Telling Us

Google reviews consistently point to:

* Trust in the people running the place ("Paul's a straight up guy")
* A relaxed, unpretentious, genuinely social environment
* Long-standing reputation among local boat owners
* Peace of mind about vessel safety while away

Some reviews are decades old, reference past owners, or mention things not currently accurate (e.g. slipway status, specific staff). Don't lift specific quotes or claims from reviews into marketing copy without checking they're still true today.

The larger insight is:

**People trust this place because it feels like a real boat yard, run by real people, not a corporate storage brand.**

Protect that feeling. Don't polish it into something slicker than it is.

## Built for West Auckland boat owners

We've been doing this over a decade. Use current, honest figures — don't inflate them:

* 15+ years' experience
* 100+ boats currently in storage
* A long-standing local reputation

Only use these numbers where they remain accurate — check before publishing.

---

## Affordable, not cheap

Don't position as the budget option outright. The value comes from:

* Location close to home
* No marina fees or waitlists
* Slipway access included
* Flexible short or long-term terms

The message is **fair value for a working boat yard**, not "cheapest storage in Auckland."

# 7. Location Positioning

## West Auckland / Glendene

This is central to the identity and should be prominent — not just a service area, but part of who we are.

Create content around:

* Boat storage close to home in West Auckland
* Caravan storage near Glendene / Waitakere
* A slipway alternative to Auckland marinas

Use the real address (20 Akatea Road, Glendene, Waitakere 0602) and precise distance claims only when confirmed.

## Slipway

Genuine differentiator for trailer boats under 8m. Be accurate about current status (e.g. if under repair, say so plainly — don't oversell facilities that aren't currently operational).

---

# 8. Opportunities We Should Not Chase

## Corporate self-storage positioning

Don't sound like a national storage chain. We're a boat yard, not a storage unit business with roller doors and a call centre.

## Overstated security claims

Don't claim 24/7 monitored security, CCTV, or similar unless it is currently, actually in place. If a system is "coming soon," say that — don't imply it already exists.

## Marina-style luxury language

Don't chase "premium," "exclusive," or "boutique" marina positioning. That's not what we are, and it's not who our customers are looking for.

## Accessibility or all-weather undercover claims

Don't promise undercover or climate-controlled storage unless genuinely available for the relevant space. Be specific about what's outdoor hardstand versus anything covered.

# 11. Core Pillars of the Voice

**Unpretentious & Honest**
Speak directly and plainly. Use fewer words to say more. Never use buzzwords or corporate jargon.

**Ruggedly Durable**
Focus on resilience, weathering the storm, things built to last.

**Respectful of Tradition**
Lean into the history and timeless practicality of boating and the sea, without sounding like a historical reenactment.

**Community-Minded**
Treat the audience like crew members or neighbours, not leads or targets.

## Tone Spectrum

**What it is:** Gritty, steady, warm but gruff, conversational, dependable, practical, no-nonsense.

**What it isn't:** Slick, elitist, overly poetic, aggressive, fragile, corporate.

# 15. Visual Identity

## Colour Palette

* `#145693` — Deep marine blue (primary)
* `#128BFB` — Bright working blue (accent)
* `#A5D2FD` — Light sky blue (secondary / backgrounds)

Keep the palette restricted to these three tones plus neutral greys, white, and black for text. Avoid introducing decorative colours (golds, greens, reds) that push toward a "premium marina" feel — the palette should read practical and maritime, not luxury.

## Logotype

The "WEST AUCKLAND STORAGE" wordmark is a custom, uppercase, condensed sans-serif display face — tall, narrow, and bold, in the spirit of classic industrial/display faces like Impact or Bebas Neue.

* Use uppercase only for the primary lockup.
* Keep tracking (letter-spacing) tight, consistent with the condensed style.
* Don't substitute a rounded, soft, or script typeface anywhere near the logo — the mark should feel sturdy and industrial, matching the "ruggedly durable" voice pillar.
* Existing logo asset: `Artboard-1-90x106.png`

## Supporting Typography

For body copy and web text, pair the condensed display logotype with a plain, highly legible sans-serif (e.g. a workhorse grotesk). Avoid anything ornamental, script, or overly geometric/corporate — it should feel like signage at a working boat yard, not a tech startup.

## Imagery

### Use

* Real boats, caravans, trailers on the actual site
* Genuine hardstand, slipway, and yard shots
* Honest, unstaged photography — working yard, not styled lifestyle shoot
* Wide, practical shots that show scale and space

### Avoid

* Stock imagery of luxury marinas or superyachts
* Overly polished lifestyle photography that doesn't match the real facility
* Clichéd padlock/key iconography used as decoration rather than function
* Anything implying undercover, climate-controlled, or resort-style storage unless genuinely true

## Overall Visual Feel

Practical, sturdy, maritime, local. The blues should feel like open water and working boats, not a corporate finance app. If in doubt, ask: does this look like a real West Auckland boat yard, or does it look like a stock template? If it's the latter, redo it.

---

# 16. Final Filter

Before publishing anything — copy, design, or both — ask:

* Is this genuinely true of West Auckland Storage / Span Farm Boat Yard right now?
* Does it explain why a boat or caravan owner should care?
* Are we leading with a real advantage (location, price, slipway) rather than a generic claim?
* Does it attract customers the yard is actually good at serving?
* Have we avoided promising security, facilities, or services that aren't currently in place?
* Does the colour palette and typography still match the industrial/maritime feel, not a premium or corporate one?
* Does it still sound like Jeff and Andrew, not a storage chain?

If yes, publish it.
